From field guide to divination

I replaced an encyclopedic field guide with a divination game built around symbols, chance, and interpretation.

Worldbuilding material

Inhabitants, structures, artifacts, and phenomena became individual cards.

Player-led discovery

Chance determines which fragment of the world appears next.

Intentional ambiguity

Players connect incomplete fragments and imagine what lies between them.

The goal was to make worldbuilding feel experiential rather than encyclopedic.

One card, two readings

Every selection reveals part of Emora and reflects a symbolic question back onto the player.

The world

Lore layer

  • Name of the object or phenomenon
  • Short meaning
  • Its history and role in Emora
The player

Divination layer

  • Symbolic interpretation
  • Personal reflection
  • Meaning shaped by the moment of selection

Player flow

  1. Shuffle the deck
  2. Fan out the cards
  3. Choose one card
  4. Flip and reveal
  5. Read both meanings

How I made it

I moved from worldbuilding concepts to original Procreate cards, then built the shuffle-and-reveal system in Unity.

  1. 01 Develop the symbols

    Selected inhabitants, structures, artifacts, and phenomena from across Emora.

  2. 02 Draw every card

    Hand-drew every card in Procreate using one consistent visual language.

  3. 03 Build the Unity system

    Designed and vibe-coded the shuffle, fan, selection, and flip mechanics.

  4. 04 Choreograph the reveal

    Added the flip and sliding panel containing lore and the divination reading.

Presented at the Roxy Annex

Our class exhibited its final projects together at the Roxy Annex. I created a table installation where visitors could sit down and choose a reading.

A world assembled through chance and curiosity. The exhibition turned the deck into a shared experience where people could take turns, compare cards, and discuss their readings.
