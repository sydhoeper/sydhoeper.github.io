An explorable tunnel system built around a civilization that treated emotional honesty as a public good—not something to hide or feel ashamed of.

Emotions are signals, not moral failures.

The civilization of Emora believed suffering comes less from emotion itself than from shame, repression, and the belief that difficult feelings should not exist.

The premise

The Chambers of Becoming were created as safe, communal spaces for confronting emotions that people often avoid—desire, grief, rage, envy, and fear. Visitors could acknowledge what they felt, consider what it was pointing toward, and choose how to respond.

Belief

No emotion requires shame

Feelings were understood as information about unmet needs, boundaries, attachments, and change.

Practice

Make reflection physical

Architecture gave people a place to pause, enter deliberately, and sit with an emotion instead of suppressing it.

Tone

Safe, strange, and sacred

Organic cave forms, filtered light, alcoves, crystals, mushrooms, and glitter made introspection feel protected rather than clinical.

The structure

A circular foyer acts as the threshold into the psyche. Light enters from above, symbolic objects identify each emotion, and arched tunnels invite visitors to choose what they are ready to explore.

Why desire?

I focused the build on desire after revisiting the Devil tarot card. Its imagery became a way to explore the line between pleasure and compulsion—and the illusion that people have no agency over their shadow selves.

Symbol

Loose chains

The figures on the card appear bound, but their chains can be removed. Restriction is partly sustained by believing it is absolute.

Question

Pleasure or compulsion?

The chamber does not condemn indulgence. It asks when desire supports a life and when it begins to control one.

Guardian

Face the shadow self

A devil figure watches over the space as a guardian of honesty about attachment, appetite, sexuality, and control.

Core idea

The chamber does not tell visitors what they should want. It asks them to be honest about why they want it.

From sculpture to walkthrough

I built the chamber as an end-to-end 3D environment: sculpting the architecture spatially in VR, surfacing it, assembling the world in Unity, and creating a first-person route through the finished space.

Architecture

Sculpt from inside the space

Working in Medium let me shape the tunnels at human scale and think about how curves, thresholds, and alcoves would feel while moving through them.

Environment

Carry Emora’s visual language forward

Crystals, mushrooms, glittering surfaces, organic forms, and saturated color connect the chamber to the wider world.

Composition

Design places to pause

Alcoves and smaller zones break up the tunnel and create distinct moments for contemplation, indulgence, or retreat.

Result

A philosophical concept became a place I could walk through.

The final Unity scene stayed true to the original desire chamber proposal while giving it spatial rhythm, material detail, and a clear point of view. It became one of the school projects I am most proud of—and a foundation for expanding the other chambers later.
