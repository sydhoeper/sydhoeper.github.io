The installation scans a visitor’s face, generates a character, and sends that character walking through the “dirtscraper” on the large screen.

The assignment

Give every room a distinct past while staying inside Peter’s visual system and the project’s technical constraints.

Direction

One prompt per room

Peter’s AI prompts supplied the premise; I translated each one into a physical scene.

Constraint

A limited prefab kit

Repeated objects had to feel fresh across hundreds of spaces.

Goal

Make people wonder

Every room needed enough evidence to provoke: “What happened here?”

How I built it

I moved from environmental storytelling at room scale to cinematic composition across the finished world.

  1. Interpret the prompt

    Find the room’s story. I used Peter’s prompt to decide who had occupied the space and what might have happened there.

  2. Stage the evidence

    Build with implication. Knocked-over furniture, sprawling clutter, and unusual object combinations made each room feel interrupted rather than simply decorated.

  3. Stretch the kit

    Reuse creatively. I recombined a limited prefab library in new ways and used the team’s Meshy AI workflow when a story truly needed another object.

  4. Review side by side

    Protect Peter’s vision. Working closely in his Missoula office let us adjust rooms quickly while leaving room for my creative suggestions.

  5. Compose the interstitials

    Turn rooms into moving images. After all 200+ spaces were filled, I placed two cameras in each room and composed slow, visually compelling clips for the installation slideshow.

What I delivered

A populated world with enough specificity to support both active character exploration and the quiet interstitial moments between visitors.
