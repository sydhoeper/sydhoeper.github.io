Background

A nonlinear path to better products.

I started with physical products, moved into digital systems, and kept following the work toward harder, more human problems. Each chapter sharpened a different part of how I design today.

  1. Physical products taught me constraints

    At Giant Loop Moto, I designed off-road motorcycle gear where materials, manufacturing, and real-world use made every decision tangible.

  2. Startups taught me ownership

    At Trip Pilot, I became the sole product designer and learned to guide an evolving product from research and concept through launch.

  3. Platforms taught me systems thinking

    At Active911 and Roadpass, I led complex tools for first responders and travelers, balancing business goals with high-stakes workflows.

  4. Public service made the stakes personal

    At Ad Hoc, I improved federal services for Veterans Affairs and Social Security—work that deepened my commitment to accessible, equitable design.

How I work

Clarity is a team sport.

My role is rarely just to make an interface. I help teams understand the problem, make intentional tradeoffs, and stay connected to the people affected by what we build.

01

Find the real problem

I use research, service mapping, and systems thinking to separate root causes from visible symptoms.

02

Build shared clarity

I bring product, engineering, stakeholders, and users into the work early so decisions hold up later.

03

Make it work for more people

I treat accessibility and inclusive design as core product quality—not a checklist at the end.
