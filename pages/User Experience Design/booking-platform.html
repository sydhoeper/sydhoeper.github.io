User Experience Design · Campendium · 2022

Campground Booking Platform

Creating and integrating a complete booking experience so campers could reserve sites directly through Campendium.

Overview

Campendium wanted to generate new revenue by letting campers book sites directly on its website rather than redirecting them to third-party platforms. I designed the full booking flow from scratch and integrated it into the existing product.

Role

Product designer responsible for the end-to-end experience.

Timeframe

Approximately two months in 2022.

Tools

Figma and UserTesting.com.

Skills and responsibilities

User research, competitive analysis, workflow definition, wireframing, low- and high-fidelity design, interaction design, prototyping, developer collaboration, and annotated handoff.

The problem

Campendium was a leading campground listing and reviews platform, but it lacked direct booking. The challenge was to build a seamless experience for finding a campground, selecting a site, entering trip details, paying, and receiving confirmation.

Customer need

Find and reserve a compatible campsite without leaving Campendium or navigating a confusing third-party flow.

Business opportunity

Create a new revenue stream through a trusted, integrated campground marketplace.

Defining the work

I worked closely with my product manager to turn a very large initiative into manageable releases, agree on the order of operations, and identify clean breaking points for multiple sprints.

Step 1

Campground search

  • Identify bookable campgrounds
  • Initiate availability search
Step 2

Campsite search

  • Choose dates and guest count
  • Compare available sites
Step 3

Checkout

  • Guest, equipment, and payment details
  • Review and purchase
Step 4

Confirmation

  • Success state
  • Confirmation email

Research and synthesis

I began by understanding the existing off-platform booking journey, comparing established booking services, and turning a large set of notes and questions into an inventory of required workflows.

Existing experience

Compile context

I walked through the current third-party integration to understand what campers experienced. It was cumbersome, inconsistent with Campendium, and unclear about where the booking would happen.

Competitive analysis

Study multiple booking models

I reviewed Recreation.gov, Airbnb, KOA, and other overnight-booking products. The research showed there was no single expected path, giving us room to build a flow around Campendium’s context.

Synthesis

Inventory every workflow

I translated the research into a general flow, a workflow inventory, and a focused list of open questions to align on with the product manager before design began.

Journey mapping

Prepare the overarching flow

I mapped every step from discovering a campground to purchasing a site, then used those insights to define the full booking journey across search, selection, checkout, success, and email confirmation.

Designing the experience

The booking journey was designed in four connected parts. Each moved from inventory and sketches through wireframes, visual exploration, stakeholder review, and developer collaboration.

Step 1

Campground search

Bookable properties needed to be immediately recognizable in map and list results, with a clear action that started the availability flow.

Sketch, wireframe, and iterate

I explored where the bookable indicator and CTA should live, translated the strongest ideas into wireframes, and refined them with stakeholders until the pattern felt native to Campendium.

Final campground-search design

A “Check Availability” CTA appeared on every bookable result, a diagonal banner made eligible properties scannable, and results logic moved bookable campgrounds to the top.

Step 2

Campsite search

The campground page needed a new place to choose dates and guests, display available sites, and communicate enough equipment details for a confident decision.

Set the stage and place the flow

I inventoried everything the page required, sketched possible placements, and created a rough search flow before working with the team to choose a location within the existing campground page.

Build a campsite-card pattern

I extended Campendium’s existing cards to preserve visual consistency while adding site imagery, price, capacity, equipment dimensions, hookups, access type, amenities, and a Reserve action.

Final campsite-search prototype

The final interaction combined a responsive availability form, date-range selector, and detailed result cards. I worked closely with engineering to get the date picker’s behavior and visual details right.

Step 3

Checkout

The checkout needed to collect personal, equipment, and payment information while keeping campground details, policies, and pricing visible enough to support a confident purchase.

Research and inventory

I reviewed checkout models from Expedia, Orbitz, Airbnb, Recreation.gov, VRBO, and others, then separated what customers needed to enter from what the interface needed to show.

  • Guest contact and equipment details
  • Payment and billing information
  • Stay, site, cancellation, and price details

From three steps to one page

I initially explored a three-step checkout to reduce cognitive load. As the flow evolved, a single-page design proved clearer because customers had already completed several steps before reaching payment and benefited from reviewing everything together.

Final checkout design

The final single-screen layout lets customers enter and edit guest, equipment, and payment information while continuously seeing their stay details, policies, pricing, and total.

Step 4

Confirmation

The final part of the experience needed to reassure customers that the reservation succeeded and give them a durable record of every relevant detail.

Design a familiar confirmation

The first email wireframe closely followed the checkout layout so customers would immediately recognize the information hierarchy.

Adapt to HTML email constraints

Engineering constraints required a substantial redesign for HTML email. Through several iterations, we found a structure that worked across platforms and remained readable in both light and dark modes.

Final deliverables

After roughly two months, I delivered the golden path in three annotated phases, covering responsive desktop and mobile behavior as well as edge cases such as validation errors and unsuccessful purchases.

Results

The platform launched in May 2022. Its experience was complete, but the business could initially offer only seven registered properties and ultimately lacked the resources to recruit enough campgrounds for the marketplace to become sustainable.

Launched

The full booking platform was released as one coordinated experience in May 2022.

Limited supply

The marketplace began with seven properties across the United States because of property-management constraints.

Sunset

Following resource limitations and company layoffs, the product was eventually removed from Campendium.
