User Experience Design · Roadpass Digital · 2023

Search Results Updates

Rethinking how Roadtrippers presents search results—and helping travelers add more meaningful stops to their trips.

Background

Roadtrippers helps people plan road trips and discover places to visit. As part of a new product team, I began with discovery work to understand how the search experience could better support the company’s waypoint KPI.

Timeline

Approximately four weeks in May 2023.

Team

Two product managers, two designers, and three developers.

Goal

Increase the number of waypoints travelers add to trips.

My responsibilities

  • Product discovery focused on the waypoint KPI
  • Mobile and desktop interaction design
  • Concept development through developer handoff

Problem

The team knew travelers were not adding enough waypoints, but the barriers were unclear. We needed to discover the problems and identify small, testable improvements.

Goal

Increase the number of waypoints added to trips—the team’s primary KPI.

Unknown

Why travelers hesitated or failed to add promising search results to their trips.

Challenge

Find the barriers quickly and translate them into focused changes the team could release and measure.

Product discovery

I ran mobile-focused, unmoderated research through UserTesting.com to watch new and existing travelers use the search experience on both iOS and Android.

Tests

  • UserTesting.com
  • Unmoderated and remote
  • Mobile-focused

Participants

  • 20 total participants
  • Four studies across iOS and Android
  • New and existing customers

Insights

  • Waypoint addition needed a more streamlined process
  • Travelers needed better ways to discover appealing stops

Mobile release

The first change made waypoint actions more visible throughout mobile search results, giving travelers a clearer and more immediate path to add a stop.

Small change, measurable result

Bring the action into the results

I added clearer calls to action directly to result cards so users could add a waypoint without interrupting their search flow.

12.16%

increase in the number of waypoints added by new users after the mobile update launched.

Desktop redesign

After the mobile release, I expanded the work into a more comprehensive desktop search-results redesign focused on clarity, comparison, and accessibility.

01 · Main flow

Make results easier to scan and act on

The updated structure strengthened result hierarchy and brought the waypoint action into a consistent location throughout the list.

02 · Desktop research

Audit every action in the results

I mapped the existing “button farm” across desktop result cards to understand redundant controls, competing actions, and opportunities to simplify the interface.

03 · Ideation

Explore clearer card structures

I tested different ways to balance place information, imagery, ratings, and trip actions without overwhelming the map-and-list layout.

04 · Iteration

Carry mobile learning into desktop

I translated the strongest mobile pattern into desktop layouts and iterated across list density, button placement, content hierarchy, and map behavior.

05 · Deliverables

Prepare the system for development

The final handoff documented desktop search states, waypoint interactions, card patterns, and supporting components.

Accessibility

The redesign improved visual clarity, keyboard navigation, screen-reader labeling, touch targets, and interaction feedback across result cards.

Key improvements

  • Increased title contrast and CTA clarity
  • Minimum 48 × 48 pixel tap targets
  • Hover and selected states that do not rely on color alone
  • Logical tab order and visible focus states
  • Tooltips and accessible button names
  • Reduced cognitive load across repeated results

Outcome

The mobile release proved that focused improvements could move the waypoint KPI. The broader desktop concepts were documented and added to the backlog before organizational layoffs and restructuring paused the work.

Insights discovered

Research identified specific, approachable areas where the team could begin improving the KPI.

Mobile KPI increase

The released waypoint actions produced a 12.16% increase among new users.

Desktop foundation

A researched, accessible redesign was prepared for future prioritization.

Lessons learned

The work reinforced the value of listening closely—to teammates, developers, and the people using the product.

1

Actively listen

The concerns of teammates are important signals, especially when joining an established product.

2

Ask developers early

Frequent technical questions help expose constraints and improve the design before handoff.

3

Watch and listen

In user testing, many of the most useful answers are waiting to be observed rather than directly stated.
