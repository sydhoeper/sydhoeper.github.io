User Experience Design · U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs

PDF Digitization

Helping disabled Veterans’ families apply for health care through a clearer, accessible digital experience.

Overview

We transformed a print-and-mail application into an accessible digital service on VA.gov.

Context

Applicants completed and mailed a PDF that VA staff processed by hand.

Objective

Create an accessible, end-to-end digital application.

Challenge

Simplify applying without adding work for VA staff.

My role

  • Design lead and primary point of contact
  • Led research, interaction design, prototyping, and delivery
  • Contributed accessible patterns to the VA design system

Challenges and opportunities

The paper process created barriers for applicants and staff.

Challenges
Opportunities
Customer
  • Paper forms were hard to complete and mail
  • Help was difficult to find
  • Meet proven demand with a guided digital flow
  • Make support easier to access
Business
  • Manual review caused errors and delays
  • Physical mail slowed intake
  • Process more applications with fewer errors
  • Accelerate delivery with the VA design system

Approach

We worked iteratively: map the service, test early, and refine a mobile-first experience.

High-level strategy

Start small and let evidence guide the work.

Methodologies

Human-centered, accessible, and agile design.

Collaboration

Frequent cross-functional reviews and a shared decision log.

Proposed solution

A guided, mobile-first application from eligibility through submission.

Design decisions

Research shaped patterns for complex households and flexible document submission.

View 10 design decisions Hide design decisions ⌄
Mobile-first

Design for the smallest screen first

Starting small forced clear content priorities that scaled to larger screens.

Progressive disclosure

Show only what is relevant

Applicants saw follow-up questions only when they applied.

Dynamic content

Use each applicant’s perspective

Capturing the signer first let later content reflect each applicant’s role and name.

Document flexibility

Allow optional documents

The flow supported optional evidence without making it a barrier.

Upload timing

Ask for files when they become relevant

Files are requested in context and can be added later.

Address pattern

Reduce repeated address entry

A reusable-address pattern removed repeated household data entry.

List and loop

Make repeated applicants manageable

Applicants create a household list, then complete each person’s details. The pattern later informed the VA design system.

Review

Give documents a clear overview

Review groups documents by applicant and makes missing files easy to spot.

Mail-in alternative

Keep a non-digital path available

A visible mail option preserves access without blocking digital processing.

Post-submission

Make application status unmistakable

Clear alerts distinguish complete applications from those still missing files.

Outcomes

The service launched in fall 2024 and quickly demonstrated demand.

Results and metrics

  • 24,000 applications submitted online in the first two months
  • 76% of submissions were online by May 2025
  • Processing time fell from 17 minutes to under 5 by July 2025

What I learned

  • Early process alignment prevents rework
  • Deep research reveals the real service constraints
  • Prioritization keeps stakeholder needs actionable

Impact on Veterans and families

  • Benefits received in weeks instead of months
  • A guided, accessible way to apply from home

Impact on VA application processors

  • Fewer missing signatures, lost forms, and handwriting errors
  • Faster review through direct, legible submissions

A clearer digital service allows more people to apply—and helps processors approve applications faster, with fewer mistakes.
